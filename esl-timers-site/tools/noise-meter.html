About this tool

The ClassCarnival noise meter turns your computer’s microphone into a live sound-level display for the classroom. Pick the volume level that fits the activity — silent work, whispers, partner talk or group work — and students can see at a glance whether they’re in the zone. Choose from three displays: colourful bouncing balls that jump higher as the room gets louder, a big noise gauge with green, yellow and red zones, or a sleeping dragon that stirs as the noise rises and wakes up with a roar if the class goes over the limit.

Turn a quiet class into a game with the quiet challenge: every second spent under the limit counts toward the goal, and reaching it earns the class a star. The meter also counts how many times the room got too loud, and can play a gentle “shh” to remind everyone. Adjust the microphone sensitivity to suit your room, laptop or classroom microphone.

Your privacy is protected: the sound level is measured live inside your browser and nothing is recorded, stored or uploaded. It works in Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox on a laptop or desktop connected to your projector — no sign-up, no download.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Classroom Noise Meter free?

Yes. The Classroom Noise Meter is completely free — no sign-up, no student accounts and nothing to download. It runs in any modern web browser on a laptop, tablet, interactive whiteboard or projector.

Does the noise meter record my class?

No. The sound level is measured live inside your browser and nothing is recorded, saved or uploaded. The microphone switches off as soon as you close the page.

Why does it say the microphone is blocked?

Your browser asks permission the first time. Click “Allow”, or click the microphone icon in the address bar to change it. Make sure a microphone is connected — most laptops have one built in.

The meter is too sensitive — what can I do?

Lower the “Mic sensitivity” slider, or pick a higher volume level such as Partner talk or Group work. A laptop at the front of a big room may need the sensitivity turned up instead.
